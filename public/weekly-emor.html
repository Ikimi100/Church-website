Messianic Insights

אמר — Emor: "Speak / Say." After laws for the priests' holiness comes the great calendar of Leviticus 23 — the moedim, the appointed times of the LORD. Sabbath, Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Weeks, Trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and Tabernacles: God's own rhythm of remembering and rejoicing.

"These are the appointed feasts of the LORD, holy convocations, which you shall proclaim at the time appointed for them." Leviticus 23:4

These feasts are not mere holidays; they are appointments God set to meet with His people, and they form a prophetic timeline. The spring feasts cluster around redemption; the fall feasts around final ingathering and dwelling with God. Israel was to keep them generation after generation as a way of telling the story.

Each appointed time finds its center in Messiah. He is our Passover Lamb, the unleavened Bread of sincerity, the Firstfruits of the resurrection. At Weeks the Spirit was poured out. The fall feasts still await their fullest unfolding — the trumpet, the atonement of a nation, and God tabernacling with His people forever.

02

Gospel Insights

Shadows of things to come. The feasts are, in the words of the apostles, a shadow of what was to come — and the substance belongs to Messiah. Passover finds its meaning in His death, Firstfruits in His rising, Weeks in the Spirit's coming. To learn the feasts is to learn the calendar of the Gospel.

There is grace woven through Emor for the broken too: the command to leave the gleanings for the poor and the stranger sits right in the middle of the feast calendar. God's appointed joy is never to forget the vulnerable; celebration and compassion belong together at His table.

Practical Application

Sacred rhythm shapes the soul. This week, build one intentional 'appointed time' with God — a set Sabbath rest, a meal of remembrance, a fixed hour of worship — and keep it as a real appointment with Him. Let the calendar itself preach the story of redemption to your household.

03

Thematic Focus

"Appointed Times" — Living by Heaven's Calendar

We tend to let the urgent set our schedule. Emor teaches a people to let God set the rhythm — regular pauses to remember what He has done and to anticipate what He will do. Rhythms of rest and remembrance are not legalism; they are gifts that keep the heart oriented to God.

Because the feasts tell the story of Messiah, keeping their meaning keeps the Gospel before our eyes through the year. The Lamb who was slain, the Firstfruits who rose, the Spirit who came, and the King who will return to dwell with us — the appointed times rehearse our whole hope.

Keep an Appointment with God

Set one fixed, recurring time this week to meet with God and guard it like any other appointment.

Receive the Gift of Rest

Take a genuine Sabbath pause — unplug from work and striving for a set time and remember the Lord who provides.

Leave the Gleanings

Build compassion into your celebration: share food or resources with someone in need this week.

Tell the Story

Explain to a child or new believer how one feast points to Yeshua — pass the testimony on.
